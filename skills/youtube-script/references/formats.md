# Script formats — seven proven shapes for any topic

Every script picks ONE format. Formats are skeletons; the topic fills them.
All timings are for a 3-minute video (scale linearly for 1 min / 8 min).

Narration beats are speech units, not unique-image slots. The measured visual
cadence has a 2.7667 s corpus median (production envelope 2.3–3.1 s), but visual
states may hold/reuse and local actions may occur without a full-frame cut.

---

## 1. myth — "The story you heard is wrong (and then it got proven right)"

For: myths, legends, folklore, "turned out to be true" stories.
Source: measured from Paint Explainer's own structure (5 acts per story).

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | HOOK | The myth in one line + a promise: "this one turned out to be real" |
| 8–30 s | THE MYTH | Tell the story fully and vividly, as people believed it |
| 30–55 s | THE DOUBT | "Of course, most people assumed it was just a myth." Name the doubters |
| 55–95 s | THE DIG | Real evidence: names, dates, the dig, the artifact, the paper |
| 95–150 s | THE EXPLANATION | Diagram beat: what actually happened, and why it became a myth |
| 150–180 s | THE KICKER | "This is likely how the story spread" + one new curiosity gap |

Multiple myths in one video = chain chapters: each ~55–60 s, one chapter per
myth, 0.7 s silence between chapters.

## 2. misconception — "What you think is true… isn't"

For: science, psychology, health, everyday beliefs. (Derek Muller's
misconception-first finding: refuting a wrong belief beats stating facts.)

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | HOOK | State the belief everyone holds, confidently |
| 10–30 s | WHY IT FEELS RIGHT | Steelman it. Why does it sound true? |
| 30–90 s | THE BREAK | The experiment/data point that breaks it; change the visual only when semantics require it |
| 90–150 s | THE REAL MECHANISM | Build the true explanation layer by layer |
| 150–180 s | SO WHAT | What changes now that you know |

## 3. mystery — "Something impossible happened. Here's how we know."

For: true stories, cold cases, historical puzzles, unsolved science.

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–12 s | HOOK | The impossible event, in one sentence |
| 12–40 s | STAKES | Who cared, what was at risk |
| 40–110 s | CLUE TRAIL | Three clues, each raising tension; states may reuse the established plate |
| 110–140 s | THE TWIST | The clue that breaks the obvious answer |
| 140–170 s | THE REVEAL | What really happened / what we now believe |
| 170–180 s | THE MEANING | Why it still matters |

## 4. how-it-works — "Build the machine in the viewer's head"

For: technology, biology, physics, economics, anything with a mechanism.

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | HOOK | The result: "This object does X every second" |
| 10–35 s | BIG PICTURE | The whole system in one diagram, parts named |
| 35–130 s | LAYERS | Explain each part; add one semantic piece at a time on the locked plate |
| 130–160 s | THE KEY INSIGHT | The one trick/idea the whole machine leans on |
| 160–180 s | IMPLICATIONS | What it means that this works |

## 5. comparison — "A vs B, round by round"

For: rival products, animals, countries, ideas, historical choices.

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | HOOK | The rivalry + who usually wins in people's minds |
| 10–140 s | ROUNDS | 4–6 categories (speed, size, cost, endurance…); score kept in semantic states |
| 140–165 s | THE DECIDING FACTOR | The one category that flips the result |
| 165–180 s | VERDICT | Winner + the honest caveat |

## 6. timeline — "One thread through the centuries"

For: history, evolution of an idea, biography.

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | HOOK | The through-line question: "How did X become Y?" |
| 10–130 s | TURNING POINTS | 4–6 era beats; each marks one date and consequential change |
| 130–165 s | THE PAYOFF | The modern world the timeline produced |
| 165–180 s | THE LOOP | Callback to the opening question, answered |

## 7. big-question — "Let's rediscover the answer together"

For: math, logic, philosophy, deep science (3Blue1Brown guided discovery).

| Time | Act | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–12 s | HOOK | A concrete, oddly specific question |
| 12–40 s | NAIVE ATTEMPT | The obvious approach — let it partially work, then break |
| 40–75 s | THE KEY INSIGHT | ONE new idea. Pause visually after it |
| 75–150 s | THE BUILD | Apply the insight step by step; each step feels inevitable |
| 150–180 s | THE GENERALIZATION | "This pattern works far beyond this example" |

## Hooks (pick by format)

| Type | First line pattern | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | "Everything you've been told about X is wrong." | misconception, myth |
| Mystery | "In [year], something impossible happened…" | mystery, myth |
| Stakes | "This one mistake costs people X every year." | how-it-works, comparison |
| Outcome | "By the end, you'll be able to explain X in one sentence." | big-question, timeline |
| Scale | "X is bigger/faster/older than you think — by a factor of Y." | comparison, how-it-works |

## The 30-second rule

Half the audience leaves in the first 30 seconds. By second 30 the hook AND
the tension must be complete, and the first image must already be interesting.

## But–therefore

Never join two sections with "and then". Every seam is "but" (new problem)
or "therefore" (new consequence). This is what makes a script feel like a
story instead of a list.

## Palette cleansers

Use the measured chapter boundary as the breathing structure: roughly 0.6–0.8 s
of intentional breath, typically around the ~68.5 s median chapter scale in
reference-length videos. Do not claim an unsourced retention effect or force a
visual gag at every boundary.
