# Topic picker — when the user has no topic

The agent proposes **3 topics** with a one-line pitch each and lets the user
tap one. Pick topics that are:

1. **Evergreen** — no news-cycle topics; interest must hold for years.
2. **Concrete** — a specific myth, object, person, event, or question. Not
   "history of Rome" (too big), but "the Roman concrete that still stands".
3. **Mismatch-shaped** — there is a gap between what people believe and what
   is true (this is the engine of misconception-first and myth formats).
4. **Provable** — real sources exist: an excavation, a paper, a court record,
   a patent, a dataset.

## Formula: subject + surprising claim

"Everyone knows X — but the real story is Y."
If Y is genuinely surprising to a normal viewer, it's a good topic.

## Example 3-picks reply (myth niche)

1. The Minotaur — the palace that may have inspired it has a room that
   matches the legend almost exactly. (myth format)
2. The Cyclops — the skulls that explain the one-eyed giant, and how they
   ended up in a museum drawer. (myth format)
3. Troy — a city burned for a woman, dug out of a hill by a man who was
   right for the wrong reasons. (myth format)

## If the user names a NICHE but no topic

Research the niche's biggest evergreen questions, then propose 3 concrete
topics with the same formula.

## If the user names a topic that's too big

Shrink it: "history of Rome" → one concrete mismatch inside it ("the Roman
concrete that still stands"). A deep, narrow story always beats a shallow,
wide one — on YouTube and in production cost.
