# Acting grammar

## Performance hierarchy

Read the shot in this order: story point → thought → eye line → head → torso →
limbs → fingers/props → hair/clothing. Motion that starts everywhere at once is
mechanical. Motion that starts from the thought reads as acting.

## Pose test

Every key pose must pass all five checks:

1. **Silhouette:** readable as a black shape at thumbnail size.
2. **Line of action:** one dominant C, S, or straight force line.
3. **Weight:** hips, planted foot, support and balance agree.
4. **Eye line:** viewer knows what the character sees.
5. **Contrast:** adjacent poses differ in shape, not merely facial features.

If a pose fails, redraw it. Do not rescue weak staging with more keyframes.

## Acting beat

Use only the phases needed by the action:

- **pre-thought hold** — viewer sees the starting state;
- **eye acquisition** — eyes often find the stimulus first;
- **anticipation** — opposite/smaller preparation;
- **breakdown** — authored path and body mechanics;
- **extreme** — clearest statement, usually held;
- **overshoot** — only for energy/material;
- **settle** — arrive at a new thought, never auto-return to neutral;
- **reaction hold** — allows narration and joke to land.

At 30 fps, a useful starting envelope is 2–5 frames anticipation, 3–10 frames
main action, 1–3 frames overshoot, and 8–30 frames readable hold. Derive timing
from character mass, emotion and aligned speech; these are not fixed presets.

## Emotion uses the whole body

| State | Shape | Face | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| confident | open chest, stable asymmetry | level brows, direct gaze | decisive ease-out |
| afraid | compressed core, limbs protect | wide eyes, raised inner brows | snap then tremulous hold |
| angry | forward wedge, lowered center | compressed eyes/brows, jaw lead | short acceleration, hard stop |
| curious | head leads, torso follows | asymmetrical brow, focused gaze | small staged offsets |
| embarrassed | inward C-curve, hidden hands | averted eyes, compressed mouth | delayed, reluctant motion |
| exhausted | broken line, hanging extremities | heavy lids, slack jaw | slow start and incomplete settle |
| delighted | lifted center, open limbs | cheek compression, eye crescents | fast expansion with soft overshoot |

## Deformation policy

- Use transform keys for rigid translation/rotation.
- Use 2–4 puppet pins for a flexible local part.
- Use a replacement drawing when silhouette, perspective, hand shape, facial
  construction, or foreshortening changes materially.
- Never stretch a neutral drawing into an extreme that should have been drawn.
- Preserve stable identifiers: skull mass, hairline, nose direction, costume
  anchors, body ratio and signature asymmetry.

## Secondary action

Secondary motion reinforces the main action and never competes with the accent
word. Root follows immediately, middle follows later, tip follows last. Hair,
coat tails and props settle after the body. Eyes can lead a turn by 1–3 frames;
hands normally lag the shoulder/elbow chain.
