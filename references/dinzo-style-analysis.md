## 1. OVERVIEW — What Makes the Dinzo Style Distinctive

Dinzo pioneered the **"Why It Sucks to Be Born as..."** format on YouTube — a subgenre of animal explainer video that presents the complete life cycle of an animal through the lens of "you are born as [animal]" second-person narration. The visuals are **crude hand-drawn / stick-figure doodles** on a white (or solid-colour) background, with:

- No camera movement or zooms
- No transitions (hard cuts only between beats)
- No character lip-sync or facial animation
- Minimal background detail
- Each beat = one static drawing on screen for 3–8 seconds
- Audio is a single male human narrator (actual voice, not TTS), deadpan delivery, ~160–200 wpm

## 2. VISUAL STYLE — Deep Breakdown

### 2.1 Drawing Technique

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Linework** | Thick, uniform black outlines on everything. Constant stroke width (~4–6px) — no pressure sensitivity, no varying thickness. Looks like a vector brush in CapCut or a digital drawing app. |
| **Figure construction** | Crude stick figures: circle/oval heads, single-line limbs (arms, legs), no hands or feet detail. Torsos are simple ovals or rounded rectangles. Anatomical proportions are rough — heads are oversized relative to bodies (chibi-like ~1:3 head-to-body ratio). |
| **Animal drawings** | Simplified cartoon animals drawn with the same thick black outlines. Anatomically basic — a horse is a rounded body + 4 stick legs + a small head. No realistic muscle definition or fur texture. Animals often have dot eyes and simple line mouths. |
| **Colour fills** | Flat, solid fills inside the black outlines. No gradients, no shading, no highlights, no texture. Colours are basic and saturated (primaries and earth tones). |
| **Backgrounds** | Almost always a single flat colour filling the frame. Most common: White (`#FFFFFF`), Cream (`#FFE0AC`), Sky Blue (`#5FBCE4`). Background colour sometimes shifts to signal mood or environment change. |
| **Labels/text** | Hand-lettered ALL-CAPS text in the same thick black line style. Labels are brief (1–5 words), placed directly on the illustration. No serifs, no fancy typography — looks handwritten with a marker. |

### 2.2 Composition & Layout

- **Centred framing** — the main subject/character is almost always dead centre in the frame
- **Minimal elements** — typically 1–3 elements per frame. Lots of negative/empty space
- **No background scenery** — no detailed environments. At most a ground line, a tree branch, or a simple horizon
- **Flat 2D** — no perspective, no depth, no shadows. Everything is on the same visual plane
- **Aspect ratio** — 16:9 landscape (standard YouTube widescreen)

### 2.3 Thumbnail Style (Separate from Video Art)

Dinzo's thumbnails are a **completely different register** from the video internals:

- **Bold, saturated, dramatic** — not doodle-style at all
- Photorealistic/3D rendered animal face or silhouette, typically menacing
- Heavy use of **red, orange, black** — danger colours
- The animal is centred, often with open mouth / teeth
- Title text in bold sans-serif: "Why It Sucks to Be Born as a [ANIMAL]"
- Thumbnails look AI-generated or sourced from stock 3D renders

This is a deliberate bait-and-switch — the thumbnail promises drama, the video delivers deadpan comedy through crude drawings.

| Colour | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Black | `#000000` | All outlines, text labels |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Default background, neutral scenes |
| Cream | `#FFE0AC` | Land, savanna, warm environments |

## 3. ANIMATION TECHNIQUE

### 3.1 What Moves (and What Doesn't)

**Static elements (most of the frame):**
- Characters in their default pose
- Backgrounds
- Environmental objects

**Animated elements (minimal):**
- **Simple limb movement** — an arm rising, a leg stepping (1–3 keyframes)
- **Object slide-ins** — a new element (predator, food item) slides in from screen edge
- **Scaling** — a threat grows larger (approaching)
- **Fade-in/fade-out** for new elements appearing
- **Bounce/wobble** — light squash-and-stretch on impact or emphasis

### 3.2 Motion Characteristics

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Style** | Keyframe animation, likely done in CapCut mobile/desktop |
| **Frame rate** | 30 fps (standard) |
| **Easing** | Simple ease-in/ease-out — not bezier/naturalistic |
| **Motion complexity** | Very simple — 1–3 moving parts per animated beat |
| **Speed** | Moderate — movements take 0.5–1.5 seconds |
| **Transitions** | Hard cuts only between beats. No wipes, dissolves, or fancy transitions. |
| **Pacing** | Each illustration holds for 3–8 seconds while narration plays. When the next beat arrives: hard cut to next drawing. |

### 3.3 Typical Beat Structure

```
[3-5 sec static drawing with narration]
    → new element slides in / character moves
    → [hold for narration to finish]
    → HARD CUT to next beat
```

### 3.4 Tools Used (Inferred)

Based on community analysis, tutorials, and the visual characteristics:

- **Drawing:** Likely hand-drawn in a vector/digital art app (Procreate, Adobe Illustrator, or directly in CapCut's drawing tools)
- **Animation:** CapCut (mobile or desktop) — keyframe-based position/scale/rotation animation
- **Voiceover:** Recorded with a microphone — Dinzo uses his actual human voice (not AI TTS, unlike some clones)
- **Assembly:** CapCut timeline — layer still images, animate with keyframes, overlay audio

> **Note:** The style is widely considered to be a **"YouTube automation" format** — the creator likely scripts the narration, records audio, then either draws the frames himself or hires freelance artists on Upwork/Fiverr to produce the doodles in bulk.

## 4. CONTENT & NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

### 4.1 Video Formula

Every Dinzo video follows the same rigid structure:

1. **Hook** — "You're born..." (immediate second-person immersion)
2. **Birth/hatching** — The animal enters the world, usually abandoned by parents
3. **Early survival** — Learning to hunt, hide, avoid predators
4. **Juvenile stage** — Growing, first kills, establishing territory
5. **Mating** — Courtship rituals, reproduction
6. **Adulthood** — Peak of power, defending territory
7. **Old age/Death** — Decline, final confrontation with predator or nature
8. **Closing** — Often a darkly humorous or poignant ending

### 4.2 Narrative Voice

- **Second person ("you")** — "You're born on Komodo Island..."
- **Deadpan, darkly humorous** — "Your mother doesn't see the difference between giving birth and pooping"
- **Colloquial and conversational** — "Good luck with that", "Spoiler: you're not"
- **No music** — just the narrator's voice and natural silence
- **No sound effects** — the narration is the only audio

### 4.3 Video Length

- ~8:04 per video (remarkably consistent)
- Covers the entire life cycle of the animal
- ~1,600–2,000 words per script

## 7. STYLE RULES SUMMARY (for replication)

### Must-Haves

1. **Thick uniform black outlines** on every element
2. **Flat, solid colour fills** — no gradients, no shading, no highlights
3. **Crude stick-figure proportions** — circle heads, single-line limbs, oversized heads
4. **Simple animal shapes** — rounded bodies, dot eyes, minimal detail
5. **Single flat background colour** per beat (white, cream, sky blue most common)
6. **Minimal elements per frame** — 1–3 elements, lots of empty space
7. **Hand-lettered ALL-CAPS labels** in thick black lines
8. **Hard cuts** between beats — no transitions
9. **Minimal animation** — limb movement, slide-ins, scaling (1–3 moving parts per beat)
10. **Deadpan second-person narration** — no music, no sound effects

### Must-Not-Haves

- No gradients or shading
- No detailed backgrounds or environments
- No camera movement or zooms
- No transitions (wipes, dissolves, fades between beats)
- No lip-sync or facial animation
- No music or sound effects
- No complex multi-character scenes
- No realistic anatomy or proportions
