## What performs

The strongest titles make a complete, familiar promise with a human frame:

- **Entire story/history:** a broad topic compressed into roughly 13 minutes.
- **Your life / every rank:** a numbered progression with built-in escalation.
- **POV / what it was like:** sensory immersion through one person's ordeal.
- **Why X failed:** a causal mystery whose answer is delayed and accumulated.

In this snapshot, the Odyssey, German-rank, German-soldier, and WW1 overview
videos are the largest. The shared traits are a highly legible promise, familiar
search subject, immediate contradiction, and a structure viewers understand in
seconds. This is correlation, not proof of causation.

## Script fingerprint

### Hook: 25–45 seconds

The timeline videos open with:

1. one scale statistic;
2. a deliberately plain causal reversal (often “but the whole thing…”);
3. three surprising preview facts;
4. a single “So, how…?” question;
5. immediate movement into the story.

Example pattern, not copy:

> This event changed X lives. But the chain began with a decision that looked
> harmless. A, B, and C followed. So how did one choice create the outcome?

The second-person videos often cold-open inside danger, then rewind:

> You hear/feel/smell the threat. You have seconds. But this is not yet the
> worst moment. Go back N hours.

### Body

- Narration is brisk, plain-language, and causal: **because → therefore → but**.
- A new concrete fact, reversal, joke, quote, or visual target arrives roughly
  every 1–2 sentences.
- Dates and quantities are anchored to a map, object, person, or consequence.
- Short imagined dialogue converts diplomatic and command decisions into
  understandable conflict. It must be clearly dramatized, not presented as a
  verbatim quote unless sourced.
- Humor is dry and brief; it releases pressure without making suffering the
  punchline.
- “Right here,” “over here,” and directional language reveal that maps and
  pointing animation carry much of the exposition.
- The second-person episodes use sensory anchors: temperature, smell, weight,
  time, texture, sound, bodily fatigue, and constrained choices.
- Rank episodes use repeated modules: age → role → pay/equipment → burden →
  survival/promotion → next level.

### Ending

The channel generally completes the timeline or personal fate instead of
switching to an abstract self-help lesson. The emotional payoff comes from the
accumulated cost, a callback to the hook, or the contrast between an individual
and the historical machine around them.

## Original visual system to build

The channel describes its work as using Paint. Reproduce the *production
principles*, not its exact assets:

- 16:9 horizontal canvas, intended for standard YouTube.
- Hand-drawn, deliberately simple figures and equipment.
- Flat color fills, dark outlines, limited shading, readable silhouettes.
- Maps with borders, arrows, highlighted regions, and labels synchronized to
  “here/there” narration.
- Recurring character sheets so uniforms, faces, and proportions remain stable.
- Staged movement rather than full animation: slide, pop, bob, shake, reveal,
  camera pan, and slow zoom.
- Frequent hard cuts and visual substitutions; avoid long static holds.
- Use documentary inserts only when licensed/public-domain and recorded in a
  source ledger.
- Keep on-screen wording short. Let narration carry detail.

### Recommended cadence

- 1280×720 drafts; 1920×1080 finals; 24 or 30 fps.
- Visual beat every 2.5–5 seconds.
- Micro-motion inside a beat every 0.5–2 seconds.
- Maps receive sequential arrow/label reveals, not a single overloaded frame.
- Use hard cuts by default. Reserve dissolves for time/place changes.
- Target 1,650–2,100 spoken words for 10–13 minutes depending on selected voice.

## Four production templates

### `entire-history`

Hook montage → necessary prehistory → trigger → chronological escalation →
turning points → resolution → cost/legacy. Use maps heavily.

### `battle-cause`

Outcome in first sentence → apparent explanation → logistics/geography/command
constraints → battle phases → decisive failure mechanism → counterfactual kept
brief and clearly labeled.

### `pov-ordeal`

Cold-open danger → rewind → identity and equipment → routine → first warning →
constraint closes → irreversible event → immediate fate → historical context.
Do not invent a named composite person's private thoughts as historical fact.

### `rank-ladder`

Level card → age/pay/equipment → responsibility → distinctive danger → survival
or replacement → next level. Escalate moral and command burden, not only salary.

## Accuracy and ethics guardrails

1. Maintain `sources.json` for factual claims and asset licenses.
2. Separate sourced quotations from dramatized dialogue.
3. Mark composite POV characters as composites in description/end matter.
4. Avoid fabricated precision, especially casualty figures and private moments.
5. Treat atrocities and death seriously; do not use victims as visual comedy.
6. Use public-domain, licensed, commissioned, or generated-original assets only.
7. Do not use the channel name, logo, thumbnails, voice clone, or exact drawings.
8. Add a description note that the video is educational entertainment and not a
   primary source.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] The title makes one clear promise.
- [ ] The hook contains scale, reversal, previews, and a question or cold-open.
- [ ] Every paragraph has a visual plan.
- [ ] Every “here/there” line has a map action.
- [ ] No shot is static for more than five seconds without a reason.
- [ ] Character identity stays consistent across scenes.
- [ ] Claims and media are represented in the source ledger.
- [ ] Dialogue is sourced or labeled as dramatization.
- [ ] Draft is checked at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 95% timestamps.
- [ ] Final audio is intelligible, normalized, and free of clipping.
