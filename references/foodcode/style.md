# FOODCODE Style — the measured formula (from @Foodcode1 transcripts)

Extracted from the channel's own scripts ("Broccoli Shock", "Mushroom Shock").
Every new food video should repeat this arc exactly. The repetition IS the format.

## Title formula

"[Subject] Shock: This Is What [FOOD] REALLY Do To Your [BODY SYSTEM]"
- "Broccoli Shock: This Is What Broccoli Sprouts REALLY Do To Your DNA"
- "Mushroom Shock: This Is What Mushrooms REALLY Do To Your Immune System"
- (variant) "Dark Chocolate: What REALLY Happens In Your Brain"

Pattern: concrete food + a body system people never connect to it (DNA, immune
cells, brain) + the word "REALLY" in caps. The gap between "what you think food
does" and "what it really does" is the click.

## The 12-beat arc (every video, in this order)

1. **Cold-open fact** — a specific, measured, surprising number, stated flat.
   "Scientists exposed human immune cells to a compound... Natural killer cell
   activity increased by up to 300%." Open with the mechanism, not the food.

2. **Retention promise** — "Stay until the end because I'm going to show you
   exactly [the thing] and [the mistake that destroys it]." Then:
   "This is not about [obvious thing]. This is about [deeper thing]."

3. **Relatable setup** — "Think about the last time you [bought/cooked X]..."
   Paint the viewer's own ordinary habit. Validate it ("You got some nutrition
   from it."). Then: "But here is what nobody told you at that [store/kitchen]."

4. **The reveal** — the gap between habit and truth. State the "mistake" that
   destroys the benefit ("never cook them", "never boil and discard the water").

5. **Name the compound + magnitude** — sulforaphane / beta-glucans /
   ergothioneine, with exact numbers ("100 times", "200 genes", "300%").
   Repeat the number for emphasis ("100 times. 100 times.").

6. **Mechanism + analogy** — "NRF2 is your body's master defense switch. Think
   of it like the circuit breaker panel in your house." / "Your immune cells are
   the firefighters. Beta-glucans are the alarm."

7. **The study** — named journal, year, human subjects, and the hammer:
   "Not in mice, not in a petri dish, in humans." (or "in healthy adults with no
   prior immune dysfunction").

8. **Second mechanism ("the part that shocked even the researchers")** — a
   second compound or second pathway that raises the stakes mid-video (HDAC +
   methylation; ergothioneine + OCTN1). Include the "nobody will tell you this"
   framing.

9. **Three steps** — "So what do you do with this information? Three steps.
   Simple. Specific." Step one / step two / step three, each concrete and
   actionable, with the "never" mistake called out again.

10. **Mission line** — "This is information in the scientific literature, but
    almost nobody communicates it to the people who need it most. That is what
    Food Code exists to do."

11. **Transformation close** — "After today, you are no longer someone who
    [X]. You are someone who understands [compound → mechanism → consequence].
    That changes every decision you make in the [aisle/kitchen]."

12. **CTA + next-video tease** — "Tell me in the comments [specific question].
    I read every single one. In the next video, I'm going to show you what [X]
    does to [Y]. Not [Z], not [W], your [Y]."

## Voice & tone

- Second person "you", calm, clinical, confident — a science narrator, not a
  hype man. Deadpan and relentless.
- Short declarative sentences. Fragments for emphasis. ("Not a drug, not a
  supplement." / "No enzyme, no sulforaphane.")
- Repetition as a device ("100 times. 100 times.")
- Specific numbers, named compounds, named journals, years (PNAS, Journal of
  the American College of Nutrition 2015, Cancer Prevention Research).
- "Never" absolutes ("never cook them", "never boil and discard the water").
- No music cues, no jokes, no slang. Pure mechanism.

## The winning-topic test (same engine as before, food-shaped)

A topic qualifies only if it has ALL FIVE:
1. **One named compound** (sulforaphane, bromelain, allicin, capsaicin…)
2. **One named mechanism/target** (NRF2, dectin-1 receptor, TRPV1…)
3. **A misconception** — what everyone believes is wrong
4. **A "preparation mistake"** — the everyday thing that destroys the benefit
   (this is the channel's signature twist)
5. **A human study** with a named journal to cite

## Length / cadence

- ~8 min, ~1,100-1,200 words → ~110-120 beats → 12 parts of 10 (as before).
- One spoken clause = one beat = one image. Hard cuts. 16:9. No captions.
