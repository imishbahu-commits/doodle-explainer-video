# Why You Cannot Tickle Yourself — expressive acting sample

Try to tickle yourself. Go on. Your fingers touch the same skin, but almost nothing happens. That is not because your hands are bad at tickling. Before you move, your brain sends a prediction to a region called the cerebellum. It estimates where your fingers will land and turns down the expected sensation. Now let someone else try. Their timing, pressure, and direction are unpredictable. The prediction misses, the alarm stays loud, and suddenly you are twisting like a malfunctioning noodle. Your brain did not stop the touch. It spoiled the surprise.
