# Why It Sucks to Be Born as a Male Honeybee (Drone) — Research Notes

Every claim in `script.md` traces to a line here.

## Genetics / birth
- Drones develop from UNFERTILIZED eggs (haploid): a mother, a grandfather, but
  no father. Fertilized eggs → female workers/queens.
  — beesafebeeremoval.com "A Bee's Life"; Orkin "Mechanics of Honey Bee Mating".
- Drone development: 24 days egg→adult (workers 21, queen 16) — the slowest.
  — beesafebeeremoval.com; kowalskimountain.com.
- Drones are larger than workers and get a bigger cell.

## The freeloader
- Drones have NO stinger, no pollen baskets, can't forage, can't feed themselves;
  fed mouth-to-mouth (trophallaxis) by workers; eat ~3x what a worker eats.
  — beesafebeeremoval.com ("a drone's singular purpose is to mate");
    scienceinsights.org ("They don't forage…").
- Only hive contribution: help regulate temperature by fanning wings.
  — kowalskimountain.com.

## The waiting
- Drones gather at "drone congregation areas" (DCAs) high in the sky, drawing
  males from dozens of colonies; nobody fully knows how they locate them.
  — beesafebeeremoval.com; scienceinsights.org.
- A queen mates once in her life, on mating flights, with ~10–20 drones
  (sources vary: "10 to 20", "more than 7", "seven to ten"). She stores
  millions of sperm (5–6 million) for life.
  — beesafebeeremoval.com; Orkin; bootstrapbee.com.
- The overwhelming majority of drones never mate.

## The mating (fatal)
- Mating lasts ~2–5 seconds. On ejaculation the endophallus (≈ the size of his
  own abdomen) everts and is ripped out, stays lodged in the queen as a
  "mating sign"; the drone falls paralyzed and dies within minutes.
  — scienceinsights.org; beesafebeeremoval.com; kowalskimountain.com.
- The eversion makes an audible "pop" — beekeepers report hearing it.
  — scienceinsights.org ("explosive, involuntary event").

## The eviction
- In autumn the colony stops feeding drones, workers bite their wings and drag
  them out ("fall drone discharge"); evicted drones can't feed themselves and
  die of starvation/exposure within hours–days.
  — scienceinsights.org; beesafebeeremoval.com; kowalskimountain.com.
- Drones are cold-fragile: ~76% died after 2h at 4°C in tests, vs 0 workers.
  — scienceinsights.org.

## Predation
- Drones are picked off by birds, dragonflies, etc. at congregation areas.
  — scienceinsights.org (Aging Cell biodemographic analysis).
