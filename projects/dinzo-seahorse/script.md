# Why It Sucks to Be Born as a Seahorse

> Paint Explainer style, professional cut: one spoken beat = 12–16 words
> (~2–6 s) = one shot. Painted flat-colour scenes (no white backgrounds),
> NO text/captions, character rigs + camera keyframes on every beat.
>
> Sources: Britannica (Why Do So Few Baby Seahorses Survive? — <1 in 200,
> 100–2,000 eggs per brood, 2–4 week pouch gestation), HowStuffWorks
> (male pregnancy, placenta-like pouch, 24-day incubation, labor via
> skeletal muscle contractions), oceanbites (dad releases 5–2,500 babies,
> 0.5% survival, abandoned after birth), DoofyDoodles/Reddit field notes
> (morning dance, 5–10 s egg handoff, dad may eat newborns).

## Section 1 — The Pouch (beats 1-5)

You are born inside your father's pouch. Not your mother's. Your father's.

In the seahorse world, the dad is the one who gets pregnant.

Your mother pressed two thousand eggs into his belly, one by one.

The moment she finished, she swam away. Her job was done.

So your father carries you for weeks, swaying in the gentle current.

## Section 2 — The Birth (beats 6-10)

Then the day comes. The birth. And it is not gentle.

He grips a strand of seaweed and squeezes, pushing for hours.

Two thousand tiny seahorses explode into the water like confetti.

You are one of them. Smaller than a fingernail. Nearly invisible.

Congratulations. You are born. Now comes the hard part.

## Section 3 — The Drift (beats 11-15)

You are the worst swimmer in the entire ocean. Not an exaggeration.

Your tiny fin beats seventy times every second, just to move nowhere.

The current owns you now. It drags you wherever it wants.

Everything with a mouth wants to eat you. Crabs, fish, sea turtles.

Your father is already gone. He does not look back. He cannot help you.

## Section 4 — The Hunger (beats 16-20)

You have no stomach. Not a real one. Food passes straight through you.

So you eat constantly, all day, every day, just to stay alive.

You need three thousand tiny shrimp every single day. Three thousand.

You hunt with your snout, sucking in anything smaller than you.

Most of your siblings starve in the first week. You keep eating.

## Section 5 — The Survivor (beats 21-25)

If you survive, you grow. You find a patch of seaweed to call home.

Your skin changes color to match the coral, the rock, the leaves.

One morning, another seahorse finds you. And you start to dance.

Every morning, the two of you dance together, tails linked, colors glowing.

Seahorses mate for life. You just found yours. Congratulations.

## Section 6 — The End (beats 26-30)

But the ocean is still hungry. Storms, trawlers, and plastic bags.

Millions of seahorses are caught every year for old medicines.

Your partner gives birth to two thousand more tiny seahorses.

Less than one percent will survive. You beat those odds once.

Out of two thousand, maybe two make it. This time, one of them could be you.
