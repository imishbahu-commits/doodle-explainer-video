# Why It Sucks to Be Born as an Anglerfish — Research Notes

Every claim in `script.md` traces to a line here.

## Who
- Deep-sea anglerfishes = suborder Ceratioidei (~160 species). Midnight zone, ~1,000–4,000 m.
  — Yale / Current Biology 2024 (Brownstein et al.); NHM London.
- Extreme sexual dimorphism. Female Krøyer's deep-sea angler (*Ceratias holboelli*) up to ~77 cm; male ~1.3 cm (~60:1). Smallest vertebrate male: *Photocorynus spiniceps* ~6.2 mm.
  — Pietsch, *Oceanic Anglerfishes*; NHM.

## Birth / larvae
- Females release eggs in a gelatinous raft that floats toward the surface. Thousands to hundreds of thousands of eggs depending on species.
  — Pietsch; FAO / fishbase species accounts.
- Larvae live in the sunlit (epipelagic) zone, look nothing like adults (no lure), then metamorphose and sink into the deep.
  — Pietsch; Smithsonian Ocean.

## Midnight zone
- No sunlight. ~2–4 °C. Pressure of 100–400 atmospheres.
- Food is scarce: "marine snow" (dead organic rain) plus rare live prey.
- Most larvae never make it. Deep-sea fish larval mortality is extreme; ceratioids are rarely collected as juveniles.

## Female
- Lure = esca on a modified first dorsal-fin spine (illicium). Light from symbiotic bioluminescent bacteria in the bulb.
  — NHM; Smithsonian Ocean.
- Sit-and-wait predator. Huge mouth, long teeth, highly distensible stomach — can swallow prey larger than herself.
  — Smithsonian; Australian Museum (*Melanocetus*).
- Meals can be weeks–months apart. Energy budget is the whole game.

## Male
- No lure. After metamorphosis many species do not feed; they live on reserves while searching.
  — Pietsch; Regan 1925.
- Hypertrophied nostrils / olfactory system to smell species-specific female pheromones in the dark.
  — NHM; Mental Floss / Regan.
- Most males never find a female. They starve, a few centimetres long, having never reproduced.

## Fusion ("sexual parasitism")
- Male bites the female's side. In facultative species he later lets go. In obligate species he fuses permanently: skin joins, circulatory systems anastomose, he is fed by her blood.
  — Yale 2024; NHM; Regan 1925.
- Eyes, fins, gut and much of the brain atrophy. He becomes a sperm-producing appendage.
  — NHM; William Beebe 1938.
- Multiple males can attach to one female (*Haplophryne mollis*, *Photocorynus*).
  — Discover Magazine / Brownstein; Pietsch.
- Fusion is possible because ceratioids lost key adaptive-immunity genes (RAG etc.), so the female does not reject the male as foreign tissue.
  — Swann et al., Science 2020; Yale 2024.

## Evolution
- Sexual parasitism evolved as ceratioids moved from shallow benthic habitats into the open deep sea ~50–35 million years ago (around the PETM).
  — Brownstein et al., Current Biology 2024.
