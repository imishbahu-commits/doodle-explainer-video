# Why It Sucks to Be Born as an Anglerfish

## Section 1 — The Beginning (0:00–1:00)

You are born in a floating raft of eggs, drifting at the surface of the open ocean. Your mother is already a thousand meters below you. And she is not coming back.

She dumped tens of thousands of you in a jelly raft, then swam away. You are not special. You are a number.

You hatch at the size of a grain of rice. You look nothing like the monster on the thumbnail. No fishing rod. No fangs. No light. Just a speck with a will to live.

Congratulations. You're alive.

For now.

## Section 2 — The Drop (1:00–2:30)

Everything in the sunlit water wants to eat you. Fish. Birds. Even jellyfish. Most of your siblings are gone before the first week is over.

You do the only thing a baby anglerfish can do. You drift. You eat what's smaller than you. And you hope the bigger things miss.

Then, if you're still alive, you do the stupidest thing a surface animal can do. You sink. Down past the last of the sunlight. Down into a place with a name that is not a metaphor.

The midnight zone.

There is no sun here. Ever. The water is just above freezing. The pressure would fold a human like paper. Food is whatever dead stuff rains down from above. Scientists call it marine snow. You call it dinner. If you're lucky.

Most animals down here starve.

You are about to become two completely different animals, depending on one thing. Whether you hatched female, or male.

And if you hatched male — I am sorry.

Let's start with the girls. They have it better. Relatively.

## Section 3 — If You're Female (2:30–4:00)

If you're female, you grow a fishing rod out of your forehead. It is a spine that used to be a fin, now bent forward like a pole. At the end of it is a bulb. And inside the bulb, bacteria live rent-free.

They make light. The only light you will ever see.

You sit in the dark, and you wave it. A tiny glowing lantern in an ocean of nothing. Hungry things swim up to look. That is the last decision they ever make.

Your mouth is a trap door. Your teeth are needles. Your stomach is a suitcase. You can swallow things bigger than you.

You don't chase. Chasing wastes energy, and energy is the one thing you don't have. You wait. Days. Weeks. Sometimes months between meals.

When something finally bites, you don't chew. You inhale. Your jaws unhinge. Your belly swells like a balloon. You look pregnant. You're not. You're just full.

Life as a female anglerfish is sitting in a cave of water, holding a lamp, hoping dinner is stupid. It works. You grow. You get rounder, uglier, and harder to kill. You might live for years down here.

But there is one problem even a lamp and a suitcase stomach can't solve. Finding a boyfriend. In the dark. In an ocean the size of a planet.

## Section 4 — If You're Male (4:00–5:30)

If you're male, you do not get the lamp. You do not get the jaws. You do not get the suitcase stomach.

You stay tiny. In some species, she is sixty times your size. You are a few centimeters long. She is a monster.

Your entire adult body is basically a nose. Huge nostrils. A brain built to smell one thing. A female. Her pheromones. In the largest habitat on Earth.

You cannot hunt. After you grow up, some of you never eat again. You have enough fat to last a little while. That's your clock.

You are a lottery ticket with fins.

You swim. You smell. You hope. There is no map. There is no light. There is no second chance.

Most of you never find her. You burn your last calorie in the dark, a few centimeters long. You die having never seen another of your kind.

That is the normal ending. That is what usually happens.

But suppose you get lucky. You smell her. You follow. You see a glow. There she is. A fish the size of a nightmare, with a lantern on her face.

This is the best day of your life. It is also the last day you are a fish.

## Section 5 — The Bite (5:30–7:00)

You bite her belly. You do not let go. Your teeth lock in. Your skin starts to melt into hers.

This is not a metaphor.

Your tissues fuse. Your blood vessels hook up to hers. You are now on her bloodstream. She is feeding you. You will never swim again.

Your eyes dissolve. You don't need them. Your stomach withers. You don't need that either. Your fins go. Your guts go. What's left of your brain shrinks.

You become a pair of testes with a leftover tail.

Scientists have a polite name for this. Sexual parasitism.

You are not a husband. You are a spare part.

She might already have six of you hanging off her. Little lumps. Former boys. Now just plumbing. In return, you give her sperm whenever she wants it. She never has to find a male again. She already glued them on.

This only works because her body doesn't reject you. Most animals would treat a fused stranger as a disease. Anglerfish deleted that part of their immune system. They turned off the alarm so the roommate could move in.

## Section 6 — The Deal (7:00–8:00)

When she's ready, she lays another jelly raft of eggs. Tens of thousands of them. They float up, toward the sun you will never see again.

The raft drifts. The eggs hatch. The cycle starts over. A few of those babies will sink. Almost none will make it. If they're female, they grow a lamp and wait. If they're male, they grow a nose and pray.

You, the original you, are still up there on her side. Not thinking. Not hunting. Not even really being a fish.

You found the only girl in a thousand miles of black water. And the price was everything that made you you.

As far as endings go, it could be worse. You don't have to hunt. You don't have to see. You don't have to be alone. You just have to stop being a person. Sorry. A fish.

This is the most successful dating strategy in the largest habitat on Earth. You didn't choose it. Evolution chose it, fifty million years ago, when your family moved into the dark.

The lamp. The nose. The fusion. The missing immune system. All of it, so two animals could find each other once, and never lose each other again.

Next time you see that nightmare fish with a light on its head — look closer. The tiny lump on her side? That used to be someone.

He won.
