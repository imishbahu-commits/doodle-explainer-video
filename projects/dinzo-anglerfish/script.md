# Why It Sucks to Be Born as a Deep-Sea Anglerfish

> Dinzo-style second-person deadpan explainer, WITH A TWIST: the video opens
> as if you are the famous nightmarish female anglerfish, then flips — you
> are actually the one-inch male. Landscape 16:9, crude hand-drawn doodles,
> flat colours, thick black outlines, hard cuts, one spoken beat = one image.

## Section 1 — The Dark (0:00-0:55)

You are born where the sun has never been. A mile down, in water so cold it
feels like nothing, in darkness so total that your own body might as well not
exist. This is the midnight zone. The deepest, emptiest, loneliest
neighbourhood on Earth.

Down here, there is no up and down. There is no day. There is only black,
and pressure so heavy it would crush a submarine like a soda can, and one
question that every living thing down here spends its life answering:

Is that a meal, or am I?

Because you are not born cute. You are born a nightmare. As you grow, your
head sprouts a fishing rod with a glowing bulb on the end. Your jaw fills
with rows of needle teeth, each one curved backwards, so that anything that
goes in never comes out. You don't chase food. You just open your mouth, and
the food swims in. The dark itself is your camouflage.

Congratulations. You are the most famous monster of the deep. The one in
every documentary. The anglerfish.

Now let me tell you the part they leave out.

## Section 2 — The Twist (0:55-1:55)

Because here's the thing nobody tells you in the documentaries. You're not
her.

That terrifying fish with the glowing lure and the needle teeth? That's the
female. And you are not the female. You are the other one. The one they never
film. The one that, for most of human history, scientists couldn't even
identify — they kept finding these weird little lumps stuck to the females
and assumed they were parasites. They weren't. They were you.

You are the male anglerfish. And if the female is a monster, you are a
joke. She can grow to twenty, thirty centimetres, a half-million times your
weight. You? You're about the size of your own thumbnail. You have no lure.
No giant teeth. No terrifying reputation. You are a swimming piece of lint
with a single life goal.

And that life goal is about to ruin your whole existence.

## Section 3 — The Search (1:55-3:05)

Here's your problem. You live in the biggest, darkest, emptiest place on
Earth, and you need to find exactly one thing: her. The odds are
astronomically, comically against you. The ocean is 300 million cubic
miles, and you are an inch long. Finding a mate down here is like finding one
specific grain of sand in a desert you've never seen, in the dark, while
being crushed.

So evolution gave you a plan. You're born with a giant liver — a fuel tank —
and you spend your entire adult life just... swimming. Looking. You don't
hunt. You don't really eat. You have one superpower, and it's your nose.
Male anglerfish have enormous nostrils, tuned to catch the faintest chemical
scent of a female drifting through the black.

You swim for months, burning your liver like a candle, until one of two
things happens. Either you find her... or you die trying. Most of you die
trying. There are no second chances. There is no backup plan. There is only
her scent, or nothing.

## Section 4 — The Meet (3:05-4:10)

And then — against every odd in the universe — you smell her. You follow it.
And there she is, hanging in the dark like a chandelier made of teeth, her
lure glowing, her needle jaw half open.

And here is your entire seduction strategy, the grand romantic gesture your
whole species was built for: you bite her. That's it. That's the move. You
swim up and you bite the side of her body and you hang on, with special
hooked teeth designed to never let go. Imagine showing up to a first date and
immediately latching onto their leg with your face. That is anglerfish
romance. It works.

But this is where it goes from weird to genuinely horrifying. Because you
don't let go. You can't. The moment you bite, your body starts to melt into
hers.

## Section 5 — The Merge (4:10-5:25)

The skin around your mouth dissolves. The enzymes in your jaw eat through her
skin, and the two of you — two separate animals — begin to fuse into one.
Your blood vessels reach into hers. Your hearts pump together. Her blood
becomes your blood. Her food becomes your food.

And then, slowly, you start to disappear. Your eyes dissolve — you don't need
them anymore, you're not looking for anything ever again. Your fins dissolve.
Your gills dissolve. Your stomach and your spine shrink away until there's
nothing left of you but the one part evolution decided to keep: the part that
makes babies.

You are now, officially, a permanent organ. A pair of testes with a memory.
Stuck to the side of the most terrifying fish in the ocean, like a keychain.

And the wildest part? Your body shouldn't even be able to do this. Fusing two
animals should make the immune system attack. So anglerfish deleted that part
of the immune system. Your species literally turned off the part of you that
would say no. For love.

## Section 6 — Forever (5:25-6:15)

So this is your forever now. You will never eat again. She feeds you through
your joined blood, like a mother and a baby — except you're the baby, and
your mother is a fanged nightmare with a fishing pole. You will never swim
again. Never see again. Never make a single decision again.

And you're not even special. She might have half a dozen of you hanging off
her. Maybe more. Some females have been found carrying a whole necklace of
ex-husbands, each one reduced to a little lump, all of you sharing one body
and one bloodstream and one very awkward family dynamic.

But here's the thing. You found her. In the biggest, darkest, emptiest place
on Earth, against odds that make the lottery look like a sure thing, you
found her. And now you will never, ever be alone again.

Because you physically cannot leave.

## Section 7 — The Point (6:15-7:00)

So yeah. Being born as a deep-sea anglerfish kind of sucks. You spend your
entire life as a piece of lint with a nose, swimming through the dark looking
for a monster, and if you find her, you get to spend the rest of your
existence dissolving into her side as a professional sperm dispenser.

But you know what? For an animal that never had a chance — no lure, no
teeth, no hope — you beat the odds. You survived the darkest, emptiest place
on Earth, and you found love. Literal, physical, cannot-remove-you-with-
surgery love.

And hey. Most of us spend our whole lives trying to become one with another
person. You actually did it. It just cost you everything except the one thing
you were born to keep.

Good luck out there, little fish. And if you ever feel small and useless,
just remember: somewhere in the deep, there's a fish whose entire life
achievement is being a keychain. And she loves him. Sort of.
