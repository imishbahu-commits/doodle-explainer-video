# Why Your Brain Needs Boredom

**Target:** 60 seconds  
**Format:** 16:9, 1280×720, 60 fps  
**Art:** Original hand-drawn marker and colored-pencil PNG assets

## Narration

What if doing absolutely nothing is not wasted time? The moment your phone goes quiet, boredom feels like an itch. But underneath, your brain is switching jobs. When you stop chasing new information, a set of connected regions often called the default mode network becomes more active. It wanders through memories, imagines the future, and links ideas that looked unrelated. That is why a solution can appear in the shower, on a walk, or while staring out a window. Boredom itself is not a magic creativity button. The useful part is the unfilled space after focused work, when your mind can roam without another notification grabbing it. Constant scrolling closes that space before a new connection can form. So try a tiny experiment: take a ten-minute walk with your phone in your pocket. Do not force an idea. Let your attention drift, then write down whatever returns. Sometimes your brain's best work begins when you stop giving it work.
