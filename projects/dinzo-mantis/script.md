# Why It Sucks to Be Born as a Praying Mantis

## Section 1 — The Egg Case (0:00-0:55)

You are born in a foam case glued to a twig. Not a nest, not a burrow — a
hardened blob of foam stuck to a branch in late autumn, packed with up to two
hundred of your brothers and sisters.

Your mother made it herself. She whipped liquid into foam, laid her eggs
inside, and walked away forever. That foam hardens into papery armour. It is
the only home you will ever know, and you will never meet the person who
built it.

Then winter comes. You sit inside that case through frost and snow, doing
absolutely nothing, because you don't exist yet in any way that matters. The
whole world freezes around you. You are a promise, wrapped in foam.

Spring arrives. The sun warms the twig. And one day, all of you decide at
once that it's time. You squeeze out through tiny flaps and rappel down on
threads of silk — dozens of tiny green monsters spilling into a world that is
entirely too big for you.

Congratulations. You're born. You're also breakfast.

## Section 2 — The Siblings (0:55-2:05)

Here's the first rule of being a praying mantis: your siblings will eat you.
Not metaphorically. Literally. From the second you hatch, everyone around you
is both family and food.

Hundreds of you pour out of the case together, and the first thing some of
you do is start eating each other. The people who study you have to keep baby
mantises in separate cups — because if you put two together, there's only one
by morning.

You are a nymph now, a tiny wingless copy of the adult you will become. You
already have the triangular head, the giant eyes, and those folded arms. But
you're the size of an ant, and everything wants to eat you. Birds. Spiders.
Other insects. Your own brothers and sisters.

So you scatter. You climb. You hide in the leaves and wait for something
smaller than you. The hunt starts now.

## Section 3 — The Molt (2:05-3:10)

You grow the hard way: by crawling out of your own skin. You're an insect in
armour, and armour doesn't stretch. So every time you need to get bigger, you
split your old shell down the back and pull your soft new body out of it.

You'll do this seven or eight times before you're grown. And every single
time, for a few hours after, you're a pale, soft, useless thing. You can't
run. You can't fight. You hang there, helpless, while your new skin hardens —
easy prey for ants, for spiders, and especially for any sibling that finds
you first.

You only get wings at the very end, on your final molt. Until then you're a
ground creature, a climbing creature, an ambush-in-the-leaves creature.
Patient. Still. Watching.

## Section 4 — The Hunter (3:10-4:20)

Because here's what you become when you grow up: one of the most perfectly
engineered killing machines in the insect world.

You can turn your head almost all the way around — a hundred and eighty
degrees. You are the only insect on earth that can look over its own
shoulder. That triangular head is a radar dish, and those giant eyes track a
fly across the room.

Your front legs are not for walking. They are switchblades, lined with rows
of spines, and they strike in about fifty to seventy milliseconds — faster
than a human blink. The thing you grab doesn't see it coming. It doesn't even
finish its last thought.

And then you eat it. Alive. Head first. You hold your prey in those spiked
arms and chew its face off while it's still moving. Flies. Crickets. Moths.
Bees. You'll take lizards. You'll take small snakes. Given the chance, a big
mantis will even snatch a hummingbird off a feeder.

You are the apex predator of the garden. The garden just doesn't know it yet.

## Section 5 — Mating (4:20-6:05)

And now we reach the part of your life that made you famous. Because
everything from here depends on one thing you never chose: what you hatched
as.

If you're a female, your future is straightforward. You're the biggest,
strongest thing on the branch, and when the males come to you in late summer
you have a decision to make — mate with them, or eat them. Sometimes both. In
that order. A female can be four to ten times heavier than her mate, and a
hungry female doesn't see much difference between a suitor and a snack.

If you're a male, I'm sorry. Your entire existence has been leading to this:
you must walk up to a female who is bigger than you, stronger than you, and
statistically likely to eat you. It's Russian roulette with a praying mantis.
About one time in five — maybe one in three — she decides you're the main
course.

But here's the part that keeps you up at night. Even if she bites your head
off — and she might — it doesn't stop you. Because mating isn't actually
controlled by your brain. It's wired lower down, in the nerves in your body.
So she eats your head, and your body just... keeps going. You finish the job.
Headless.

Scientists actually watch this happen. They call them the headless horsemen.

## Section 6 — The Eggs (6:05-6:55)

If you're the female, the story ends differently. After mating — whether you
ate him or not — you get to work. You don't need him anymore. You never did.

You lay your eggs in foam, the same way your mother did. A hundred. Two
hundred. Maybe more, across several cases if you can manage it. And every
male you ate becomes a little more of that foam. Literally. His body becomes
your eggs.

Then autumn comes. The first hard frost arrives. And that's it. That's the
whole thing. In the wild, a praying mantis lives exactly one season. You grew
from an egg in spring, and you die with the leaves in fall. You will never
meet your children. You'll never even know if they made it.

You just leave your foam case glued to a twig, like your mother left you, and
you trust the spring to do the rest.

## Section 7 — The End (6:55-7:45)

Because here's the truth about being a praying mantis. Your life is one long,
violent summer.

You were born packed into foam with two hundred cannibals. You grew by
shedding your skin and hiding from your own family. You became a perfect
killer — the only insect that can look back at you while it plans how to eat
you. And if you were born male, your entire purpose, your whole reason for
existing, was to find a female, give her everything, and — if she was hungry —
become her dinner while you were still finishing the job.

And the worst part? You'll do it anyway. Headless. On instinct. Because that
is what a praying mantis is.

As far as being born goes... it sucks. But hey — at least you got to eat your
brother.
