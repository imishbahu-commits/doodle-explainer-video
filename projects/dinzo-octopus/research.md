# Why It Sucks to Be Born as a Giant Pacific Octopus — Research Notes

Every claim in `script.md` traces to a line here.

## Life cycle
- Giant Pacific Octopus (*Enteroctopus dofleini*) = largest octopus; North Pacific rim.
  — SeaDoc Society: https://www.seadocsociety.org/giant-pacific-octopus-facts
- Lifespan 3–5 years (longest of any octopus).
  — Alaska ADFG: https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=giantpacificoctopus.printerfriendly
  — Ocean Conservancy: https://oceanconservancy.org/wildlife-library/giant-pacific-octopus/
- Semelparous: reproduces once, then dies.
  — thesea.org: https://thesea.org/giant-pacific-octopus-lifespan/
- Female lays ~50k–100k eggs (estimates 18k–400k across sources).
- Hatchlings ~ grain of rice (~6 mm), drift as plankton for weeks–months.
  — Alaska ADFG; SeaDoc; thesea.org.
- "from the thousands of eggs, only 1 to 2 will survive to become mature octopuses."
  — SeaDoc Society.

## The mother's vigil
- Female guards eggs ~6 months without feeding; fans water with arms/siphon.
- She starves and dies about the time the eggs hatch.
  — Alaska ADFG; Ocean Conservancy.
- Driven by the OPTIC GLAND — triggers post-reproductive senescence.
  — thesea.org.
- Senescent mothers may self-cannibalize (eat their own arms).

## Mating
- Male passes a spermatophore with a specialized arm, dies within weeks.
  — Alaska ADFG.
- Female can store sperm months before laying.
  — Ocean Conservancy.

## Body / abilities
- No bones; only hard part = beak → squeezes through any gap the beak fits.
- Three hearts; blue copper blood (hemocyanin).
- ~2/3 of neurons in arms; arms act semi-independently ("nine brains").
- Thousands of chromatophores for instant color/texture change.
  — Aquarium of the Pacific.
- Hundreds of suckers, each with taste receptors.
- Ink cloud, jet propulsion, arm autotomy + regeneration.
- Den "midden" (shell piles); tool use (coconut shells); jar opening; maze solving;
  recognizes humans, holds grudges.
- Size: avg ~12 ft / 50+ lb; large specimens ~30 ft span, hundreds of pounds.
  — Ocean Conservancy (12 ft, 50 lb).

## Prey / predators
- Prey: crabs, clams, shrimp, small fish.
- Predators: seals, sea lions, sea otters, sharks, moray eels; larvae eaten by
  nearly everything (fish, jellyfish, birds).
