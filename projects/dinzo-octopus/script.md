# Why It Sucks to Be Born as an Octopus

> Dinzo-style second-person deadpan explainer. Landscape 16:9, crude
> hand-drawn doodles, flat colours, thick black outlines, hard cuts.
> One spoken beat = one image. 10 beats, ~2:40 total.
>
> Sources: ScienceAlert (optic gland / senescence, Univ. of Chicago),

## Beat 1 — The Egg (0:00-0:18)

You are born inside an egg the size of a grain of rice. Not a nest, not a pouch — a tiny pearl glued to a rock in a dark underwater den. And you are not alone. You have a hundred thousand brothers and sisters.

## Beat 2 — The Mother (0:18-0:36)

Your mother glued every one of you to that rock herself. Then she stopped eating to guard you. For months, she got weaker and weaker. By the time you hatch, she'll be dead. You will never meet her.

## Beat 3 — The Drift (0:36-0:54)

You hatch as a tiny transparent ghost, smaller than a fingernail, with no idea what anything is. You drift into the open ocean with a hundred thousand siblings. Within weeks, almost all of them are gone. Eaten.

## Beat 4 — The Body (0:54-1:12)

You are now the statistical equivalent of a winning lottery ticket. But here's what you won: three hearts, and none of them work properly. The main one actually stops beating when you swim. So you crawl. Everywhere.

## Beat 5 — The Brains (1:12-1:30)

You have nine brains. One in your head, and eight more in your arms. Your arms can taste, smell, and think for themselves. Sometimes an arm does something and you just watch it happen. You are not fully in charge.

## Beat 6 — The Skin (1:30-1:48)

Your skin changes color in a fraction of a second — blue, red, brown, white — and you can't even control it. Your skin decides on its own. You are a walking mood ring with no say in the matter.

## Beat 7 — The Hiding (1:48-2:06)

Everything in the ocean wants to eat you. Fish, crabs, eels, other octopuses. So you hide. In a bottle. In a shell. In a crack. You are a genius with a two-centimeter body, living in fear of everything.

## Beat 8 — The Genius (2:06-2:24)

You grow fast, doubling your weight every few weeks. You're the smartest thing in the water. You can open jars, solve puzzles, and escape from tanks with the lid locked shut. Congratulations. Your reward is a one-year life.

## Beat 9 — The Expiration (2:24-2:42)

Because when you finally mate, your body flips a switch. You stop eating. You stop hiding. You wander into the open like a ghost, and within weeks, you're gone. No old age. No goodbye. Just an expiration date.

## Beat 10 — The Kicker (2:42-3:00)

Your mother starved to death so you could exist. Out of a hundred thousand eggs, maybe two survive. You lived for about a year, and you never saw her face. That's the octopus life. Congratulations. You were the lucky one.
