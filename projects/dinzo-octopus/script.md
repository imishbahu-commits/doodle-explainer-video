## Section 1 — The Egg and the Mother (beats 1-8)

You are born inside an egg the size of a grain of rice.

Not a nest, not a pouch. A tiny pearl glued to a rock in a dark den.

And you are not alone. You have a hundred thousand brothers and sisters.

Your mother glued every single egg to that rock with her own arms.

Then she stopped eating. For months she guards you without a single meal.

She grows weaker and weaker, while you wait inside your tiny shell.

By the time you hatch, she will be dead. You will never meet her.

Congratulations. You are an orphan before you are even born.

## Section 2 — The Drift (beats 9-14)

You hatch as a tiny transparent ghost, smaller than a fingernail.

You drift into the open ocean with a hundred thousand tiny siblings.

Within weeks, almost all of them are gone. Eaten by something.

Fish snap you up by the mouthful. Jellyfish sting you into a snack.

The ocean is a giant blender, and you are the smoothie inside it.

Only a lucky few drift back down to the sea floor.

## Section 3 — The Body (beats 15-20)

You are now the statistical equivalent of winning the ocean lottery.

But here is what you won. Three hearts, and none of them work properly.

The main heart stops beating when you swim. So you crawl everywhere.

You have nine brains. One in your head, eight more in your arms.

Your arms taste, smell, and think for themselves. Sometimes they do their own thing.

Your skin changes color in a split second, and you cannot control it.

## Section 4 — The Hiding and the Genius (beats 21-24)

Everything in the ocean wants to eat you. Fish, crabs, eels, other octopuses.

So you hide in a bottle, in a shell, or in a crack in the rock.

You are the smartest thing in the water. You open jars and solve puzzles.

But your reward is a one-year life, with a built-in expiration date.

## Section 5 — The End (beats 25-28)

When you finally mate, your body flips a switch, and you stop eating.

You wander into the open like a ghost. Within weeks you are gone.

Your mother starved to death so you could exist.

Out of a hundred thousand eggs, maybe two survive. You were the lucky one.
