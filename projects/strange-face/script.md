Turn off the lights. Sit close to the mirror. Stare into your own eyes. Ten minutes. Don't look away. By the end, two out of three people watch their own face deform and melt.

In two thousand ten, psychologist Giovanni Caputo ran this exact experiment on fifty people. Forty-eight percent saw a monster. Eighteen percent saw an animal. Ten percent saw a dead parent. Their own face was gone. You would assume your brain knows your own face better than anything. That is the mistake. Stay to the end, because the real reason will change how much you trust your eyes.

Your eyes are not cameras. They are prediction machines. You have never actually seen your face. You have seen your brain's best guess. Back in eighteen oh four, Swiss physician Ignaz Troxler found that anything unchanging simply fades. Your neurons get bored. In dim light, the guesses fill in the gaps. Familiar faces. Old ones. Not yours.

But here is the part that stays with you. The monster was never in the mirror. It was in the model. Bright lights, moving eyes, no illusion. Dim light and a fixed stare, and the face in the glass stops being yours. So ask yourself. When you look at the people you love, how much of them are you actually seeing? And how much is just the face your brain already decided to draw?
